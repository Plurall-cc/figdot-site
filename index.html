How it works

From draft to citation

  1. You and FigDot · onceYour design system

    You hand FigDot your brand guide, a report or a site you like. FigDot drafts the design system from it: colours, type, line weights. It checks contrast and colour-blind readability, fills what is missing, and you approve it. Every figure then wears it.

  2. YouYour data

    It stays where it is: Grist, a spreadsheet, a CSV file, a Git repository. FigDot reads the numbers itself and freezes a dated snapshot: no number is typed by hand.

  3. Your agentThe draft

    You describe the figure in a sentence. Your agent proposes the shape, the framing and the options; the numbers still come from the data.

  4. A personThe review

    Someone checks the framing, the numbers and the sources. Nothing is public by default.

  5. FigDotFrozen and published

    Each option gets a stable address, drawn in your design system without changing a number. When the data changes, the update goes back to review.

  6. EveryoneCited

    A link, an embed in an article, an image, the data: anyone can check the figure against its dated sources.

Anatomy

What a figure is made of

Around the visualisation, four modules. The figure page shows them all; when you embed or export, you choose which ones travel with it.

Figure 2 · PDO cheeses of Puy-de-Dôme
01

Framing

A title that asks the question, a subtitle that explains what the figure shows.

02

Variables and their options

What the figure lets you vary, and the options of each variable. Each option has its own numbers and its own address.

03

Legend, definitions, perimeter

How to read the figure, what the words mean, what is included and what is not.

04

Dated sources

Every file read is cited with its date and a link to its source. What is not measured reads “not measured”, never zero.

Agent

Written with an agent, verifiable without one

You describe the figure in a sentence. Your AI agent drafts it. Everything that counts stays in files a person can read and review.

What you say

“Put this data in Grist and make a FigDot figure: which protected cheese covers the most villages? Count the ageing cellars, then the farms only.”

You describe, it drafts

Shape, title, subtitle, options: the agent proposes them in FigDot's format. You correct in plain language.

Numbers never pass through the model

The agent only says where the data is. FigDot reads it itself and freezes a dated snapshot.

No AI in the rendering

Same design system, same data, same figure, at every build. That is what makes it citable.

A companion to Grist

Grist is the workshop. FigDot freezes.

A FigDot widget sits in your Grist document. Each row of the table is one iteration of a draft, with a live preview under the design system you choose.

  • Your data stays in your document.
  • One button marks a figure “to send”. FigDot comes to fetch it and dates the snapshot.
  • Once sent, the version of record is the one in FigDot.

Grist is the first companion, not a requirement: FigDot also reads a spreadsheet, a file or a repository.
